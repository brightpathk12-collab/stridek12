The Story Behind STRIDE

STRIDE wasn't built in an ivory tower. It was built by practicing educators solving real problems — and it reflects the equity imperative that AI literacy can't be optional or reserved for the privileged few.

Jacob Harvey

Jacob Harvey is a CTAE (Career, Technical, and Agricultural Education) educator who built something remarkable: LIA2, a locally-hosted AI platform that runs on school hardware. LIA2 isn't cloud-dependent. It doesn't require external accounts. It doesn't send student data anywhere. It's a complete AI platform that schools can own and operate themselves.

Jacob brings the technical platform — LIA2 defines how students practice what STRIDE teaches.

Steven brings the curriculum framework — STRIDE defines what students learn. Jacob brings the technical platform — LIA2 defines how they practice it.

This combination is unique in K-12 education. No other product pairs a comprehensive curriculum with a working, privacy-first AI platform designed specifically for school hardware.

The Gap

Why STRIDE Exists

Most AI literacy frameworks are built by academics for academics. They're theoretically sound. Philosophically robust. And almost impossible to actually use in a classroom.

STRIDE was built differently. It was built by practicing educators to solve problems they faced every day:

  • How do we ensure every student has access to AI literacy, not just advanced learners?
  • How do we teach students to use AI responsibly when we don't have a clear rubric for what that means?
  • How do we measure whether students are actually learning, not just using?
  • How do we do all of this in a way that respects student privacy and runs on existing school infrastructure?

The equity imperative: AI literacy can't be optional. It can't be reserved for honors tracks or wealthy districts. Every student, in every subject, from kindergarten through graduation, deserves to learn how AI works and how to use it responsibly.
