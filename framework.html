Deep Dive

The STRIDE Framework

Six domains of AI literacy. Three meta-competencies. One complete system for K-12.

The Six Domains

STRIDE: Six Interconnected Domains of AI Literacy

S

Sense

Understand how AI works — from pattern recognition to neural architectures. Students see AI running locally on school hardware and observe firsthand how input quality affects output quality. This domain builds foundational technical literacy: what AI can do, what it can't do, and why the distinction matters.

T

Think

Critically evaluate AI outputs: detect bias, fact-check responses, question sources, and assess reliability. The iterative revision loop built into LIA2's PROMPT cycle makes critical thinking the default behavior — not an afterthought. Students learn that accepting AI output without evaluation is not using AI; it's being used by it.

R

Relate

Examine AI's real-world impact on people, communities, equity, and power. A privacy-first architecture is itself a lesson in data ethics — students learn why their platform doesn't collect data by examining what happens when others do. This domain connects technical capability to human consequence.

I

Innovate

Create responsibly with AI through structured prompt engineering, attribution, and ethical use. Seven specialized AI agents guide responsible creation across disciplines — from writing and code to design and 3D modeling. Students learn that AI-assisted creation carries the same ethical obligations as any other form of work.

D

Decide

Know when to use AI and when not to. Students earn AI access through quality thinking — the traffic light model ensures they demonstrate readiness before receiving full agent support. This domain builds judgment: the ability to choose the right tool for the right task at the right time.

E

Empower

Build student agency: lead AI conversations, mentor peers, advocate for equity in AI access and use. Analytics built into LIA2 make growth visible — from guided play in kindergarten to responsible innovation in high school. Students don't just learn about AI; they become leaders in how their communities use it.

Woven Throughout

C3 Meta-Competencies

Critical Thinking, Creativity, and Collective Judgment are not standalone skills. They're woven into every domain, every grade level, and every student interaction.

Critical Thinking

Evaluate AI outputs rigorously — question assumptions, detect bias, fact-check claims. Every interaction with LIA2 requires students to assess what they receive, not just accept it.

Creativity

Using AI as a thinking partner to explore possibilities and solve problems in new ways. The PROMPT cycle structures creative exploration while maintaining intellectual rigor.

Collective Judgment

Making decisions together about AI ethics, equity, and impact. Students learn that AI decisions affect communities, and communities should have voice in how AI is used.

Developmental Progression

Grade Band Progression: K-2 to 9-12

STRIDE meets students where they are and grows with them from kindergarten through graduation.

K-2 Discover: "Prompting Through Play"

Young learners discover that clarity of input affects quality of output through interactive, low-pressure activities. At this stage, AI literacy is about wonder and exploration — students learn that machines follow instructions, that clearer instructions produce better results, and that AI is a tool made by people.

Activities focus on verbal prompting, sorting exercises, and guided play with age-appropriate AI interactions. The emphasis is on building comfort and curiosity, not technical mastery.

3-5 Discover: "Prompting to Create"

Elementary students move into creative work with AI while learning a simplified PROMPT cycle: Purpose + Test/Reflect. Students begin writing their own prompts, comparing outputs, and discussing what makes a "good" prompt versus a vague one.

The C3 competencies emerge naturally: students evaluate AI-generated stories for accuracy (Critical Thinking), use AI to brainstorm creative projects (Creativity), and discuss fairness in AI-generated content (Collective Judgment).

6-8 Develop: "Prompting for Collaboration"

Middle school students unlock the full 6-step PROMPT cycle. All seven AI agents become available as students demonstrate readiness through the traffic light model. The emphasis shifts from individual exploration to collaborative problem-solving.

Students learn to use AI agents across disciplines: Writing Helper for ELA, Code Coach for STEM, Design Coach for arts, and Career Research Assistant for exploratory career pathways. The 20-point rubric becomes the standard for AI interaction quality.

9-12 Drive: "Prompting with Responsibility & Evaluation"

High school students integrate the full C3 Framework. They mentor peers, lead AI ethics discussions, and drive projects aligned to college and career aspirations. At this stage, students are expected to be autonomous, responsible AI users.

Students conduct original research using AI tools, create portfolios demonstrating AI literacy growth, and engage in community advocacy around equitable AI access. The goal is not just competence but leadership.

The Thinking Protocol

The PROMPT Thinking Cycle

Every student interaction with LIA2 follows a structured 6-step protocol. Scored on a 20-point rubric, this framework transforms vague AI requests into rigorous thinking exercises.

P

Problem

Define the task

R

Research

Show your work

O

Objective

Set a goal

M

Methods

Set constraints

P

Prototype

Write the prompt

T

Tune

Evaluate & iterate

20-Point Scoring Rubric

PROMPT Step Criteria Points
P — Problem Clear, specific problem statement with context 0-4
R — Research Evidence of prior research or existing knowledge shown 0-4
O — Objective Measurable goal with defined success criteria 0-3
M — Methods Appropriate constraints, format, and scope defined 0-3
P — Prototype Well-crafted prompt incorporating all prior steps 0-3
T — Tune Thoughtful evaluation and iterative refinement 0-3

Score of 15/20 or above unlocks full AI agent access (Green light). Below 15, students receive scaffolded support (Yellow) or clarifying questions only (Red).

Access Model

Traffic Light Model

Students earn AI access by demonstrating structured thinking. The traffic light model ensures every student gets the right level of support.

Red — Clarify

Prompt is vague or shows no prior effort. The system asks clarifying questions only — no hints, no agent access. Students must articulate what they need before receiving any AI assistance.

Agent Access: None. Clarifying questions only.

Yellow — Guide

Some work shown but prompt is incomplete. The system provides hints, guiding questions, and partial scaffolding. No complete answers — students must fill in the gaps themselves.

Agent Access: Limited. Hints and guiding questions.

Green — Collaborate

PROMPT steps completed, score 15/20 or above. Full agent access with detailed support. Students have earned autonomous AI collaboration through demonstrated thinking.

Agent Access: Full. All seven agents available.

LIA2 Platform

Seven Specialized AI Agents

Each agent is purpose-built for specific learning contexts, with guardrails that maintain educational integrity.

1

Prompt Coach

Guides students through the PROMPT thinking cycle, helping them refine their prompts before submitting to other agents. The gatekeeper that ensures quality thinking.

2

Code Coach

Supports programming and computational thinking across languages and skill levels. Never writes complete solutions — instead guides students through debugging and logic.

3

Writing Helper

Assists with writing across genres and subjects. Focuses on revision, structure, and voice — never generates complete essays or bypasses the writing process.

4

Design Coach

Supports visual design, layout, and creative projects. Helps students think through design decisions and iterate on visual communication.

5

Engineering Assistant

Guides engineering design processes, prototyping, and problem-solving. Supports STEM projects with structured methodology.

6

Career Research Assistant

Helps students explore career pathways, research industries, and connect coursework to professional opportunities.

7

3D Modeling Assistant

Supports 3D design and modeling projects, guiding students through spatial thinking and digital fabrication workflows.

Safety & Privacy

Four-Layer Guardrail Architecture

LIA2's safety system is built into the architecture itself — not bolted on as an afterthought.

1

System Prompt Rules

Every AI agent operates under strict system-level instructions that define educational boundaries, prevent inappropriate content generation, and maintain pedagogical integrity. These rules cannot be overridden by student input.

2

n8n Workflow Gatekeeping

Automated workflow pipelines validate every request before it reaches the AI model. Requests are checked against educational criteria, content policies, and student access levels before processing.

3

Structured JSON Outputs

All AI responses follow structured formats that ensure consistency, traceability, and educational alignment. Outputs include metadata for teacher review and student reflection.

4

Output Filter

A final safety layer scans all generated content before delivery to students. Content that doesn't meet educational standards or safety criteria is flagged for review or blocked entirely.
